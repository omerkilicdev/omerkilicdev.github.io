The lumped wake in three dimensions

Denda Lab · Bio-Inspired Micro Aerial Vehicles
Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering, Rutgers University · part of the research page · October 2026

Eighty time steps of a four-wing discrete-vortex run, seen from the wake: every quad is one vortex ring, red for positive circulation and blue for negative, brighter for stronger. Each wing sheds ten rings per step and the four wings have shed 3,160 rings by step 80. The velocity each ring induces on every other is the cost of the method, and it grows with the square of this count. Press lump the old wake: the rings older than one flapping period fade, and each group of 200 rings (one wing, one period) is replaced by what the far field sees of it. The arrow is the group’s dipole, the sum of circulation times area vector over its rings, which is the impulse the group carries. The sphere is the smallest ball about the group’s centre that holds all its rings; the far-field expansion is accurate when this sphere is small compared with the distance to the wing. Where the group is flat enough, the second moment of area also fixes a shape, and the equivalent vortex ring is drawn as a rectangle: it carries the same impulse, sits at the same centre and has the same second moment as the 200 rings it replaces.

Reading the two cases

In hover the wake stays under the wings and the rings stretch as they age, to about ten times the size of the forward-flight rings, so the picture is a ball of long thin quads: the group spheres are as large as their distance to the body, no group is far yet, and only eight of the twelve groups are flat enough for a rectangle. The rings of one period are shed while the wing turns, so their normals fan out and their dipoles partly cancel; what is left of the group’s impulse is a small fraction of the sum of the rings’ impulses. In forward flight the wake streams away behind the body, every group older than one period is far by the far-field rule, and the groups carry almost no net impulse at all, so one small dipole stands for 200 rings. The rule behind the picture is in the research page; the equivalent ring is chosen so that the force, which is the time derivative of the impulse, has no jump at the moment of lumping.

The two wakes are the step-80 states of the runs used for the first lumped-wake tests (October 2026). Hover: advance ratio 0.20. Forward flight: advance ratio 1.02. Same wings, same stroke, same time step.
